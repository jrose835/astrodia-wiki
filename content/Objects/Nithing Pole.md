
Níðstöng was a pole carved with runes and topped with a horse's head (or sometimes the whole head mounted on a stake), erected to direct a curse at an enemy. The curse was typically accompanied by a níð verse—ritualized insult poetry that could destroy a person's reputation and, according to belief, bring supernatural misfortune upon them.

The simplest approach is that Ulfr carries the materials to create a níðstöng when needed, but it has no mechanical effect—it's purely roleplay.

**What he carries:**

- A carved wooden stake (could be part of his "trophy from an animal" from the Outlander background, or simply a quarterstaff he's carved with runes)
- A knife for carving
- Knowledge of the ritual verses

**How it works in play:** When Ulfr wants to curse an enemy—particularly one who has wronged him or his allies—he takes time (perhaps during a short or long rest) to erect a níðstöng. He carves the target's name, speaks the curse-verse, and plants the pole facing their dwelling or direction of travel.

This has no game mechanical effect, but it establishes Ulfr's character and his belief in the power of words. It could also have narrative consequences—NPCs might recognize the níðstöng and understand its significance, taking it as a serious threat or insult. Your DM might have enemies react with fear, anger, or escalation.

**When to use it:**

- After a betrayal or insult that demands response
- To mark an enemy for future vengeance
- As a warning to those who would cross Ulfr or his companions
- As part of a ritual before a major confrontation

This connects beautifully to Ulfr's arc: the idea that words have real power, that poetry can wound as surely as a blade.

## Reskinned Arcane Focus (When You Become a Bard)

When you multiclass into Bard at level 2, you'll need a spellcasting focus for your Bard spells. Bards can use a musical instrument as their focus, but the rules also allow for flexibility with DM approval.

**Proposal:** Ask your DM if Ulfr can use a carved wooden staff or pole as his Bardic focus, representing a portable níðstöng. It's not a musical instrument in the traditional sense, but Ulfr's "instrument" is his voice—the pole is simply a focus for directing that power.

**Physical description:**

- A staff or walking stick, roughly 5-6 feet tall
- Carved with runes along its length
- Perhaps topped with a carved horse head or skull (or he adds a real skull when performing the full ritual)
- Functions as a quarterstaff in combat if needed

**How it works:** When Ulfr casts Bard spells, he grips the staff, speaks his verses, and channels the magic through the carved runes. For Vicious Mockery, he points the staff at his target while delivering the curse. For Bardic Inspiration, he taps the staff rhythmically while chanting encouragement to his allies.

This is purely cosmetic—mechanically, it functions exactly like any other Bardic focus. But it reinforces Ulfr's identity as a different kind of Bard, one whose tradition comes from curse-poetry rather than lutes and love songs.
